<p>People ask for "all the data." That sentence is how a scraping project misses the date and, worse, collects something it should not. A feed is a small contract. Before any build, four things should be written down.</p>
<h2>The source</h2>
<p>Name the app or the website, and the screens or pages. "Competitor prices" is a goal. The product page, the city, and how often the price changes are the source. Mobile apps and websites are both in scope here. A login wall is not.</p>
<h2>The fields</h2>
<p>List the columns you will actually use. A price, a name, a city, a rating, an address. If a column is not on the list, it is not part of the feed. Business contact details belong on that list only when you are allowed to collect them.</p>
<h2>The schedule</h2>
<p>Say how fresh the data must be. A menu that changes weekly does not need a scrape every ten minutes. A scheduled feed that starts, collects, and stops is cheaper to run and easier to explain than a machine that is always on.</p>
<h2>The line</h2>
<p>Public pages, public app screens, and listings you have a right to use. Not passwords, not private accounts, not a database you do not own. If a request crosses that line, the project should be refused. That is a feature of the service, not a gap in it.</p>
<p>If those four lines are clear, the build is ordinary engineering. If they are not, more scraping will not fix it.</p>
