<p>Custom software is a wide door. Walking through the wrong one is how a two-week bot becomes a six-month app. The choice here is practical.</p>
<h2>A bot</h2>
<p>Telegram or WhatsApp, when the people who need it already live in that chat. Orders, alerts, internal requests, a status check. If the conversation is the product, build the bot. Do not wrap it in an app nobody will install.</p>
<h2>A desktop app</h2>
<p>When the work happens on a computer all day, with files, a device, or a screen that a browser keeps fighting. If it is a form that could be a website, it should be a website.</p>
<h2>A mobile app</h2>
<p>When the customer is away from a desk and the phone is the point: camera, location, or a habit of opening an icon. A brochure does not need an app store review.</p>
<h2>A React web application</h2>
<p>When many people, many records, and many states share one system. Dashboards, internal tools, a product with accounts and roles. This is the heavy one. It includes the backend. It should not be the default for a company that needed a clearer website.</p>
<p>If the honest answer is "our site is old," that is website modernization, not a custom platform. The cheaper correct build is the one to sell.</p>
