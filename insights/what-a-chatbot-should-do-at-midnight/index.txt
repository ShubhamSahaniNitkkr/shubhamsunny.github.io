<p>A chatbot that only repeats the FAQ is a pamphlet. The reason to put one on a website is the hour when no one is at the desk. A customer wants to move an appointment, ask if a refund is possible, or at least not be told to call back tomorrow.</p>
<h2>Put it where they already are</h2>
<p>For this practice the bot is a widget on your website. Not a new app the customer has to install. If they found you on the site, the help should be on the site.</p>
<h2>Let it act, inside rules you wrote</h2>
<p>Three actions cover most of the midnight queue: book or move an appointment, start a refund, and open a ticket. Each one needs a rule. Which appointments can move themselves. Which refunds can start without a person. What a ticket must contain so the morning shift is not guessing.</p>
<h2>Leave a record</h2>
<p>The bot is not a night receptionist you can phone. It is software. In the morning your team should see what it told the customer and what it changed. If you cannot see that, you do not have support. You have a hidden inbox.</p>
<p>Scope the bot against those three actions first. A bot that promises to do "anything a human can do" and then cannot book a table is the one customers stop using.</p>
