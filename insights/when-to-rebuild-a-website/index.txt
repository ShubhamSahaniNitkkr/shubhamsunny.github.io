<p>An old website is not a moral failure. Plenty of them still take orders. A rebuild is worth it when the site and the business have drifted apart, or when a phone makes the current site hard to use.</p>
<h2>Start with the business</h2>
<p>The first week is not a new font. It is what the business sells, what a customer is trying to do, and which pages still tell the truth. A modernization that skips this step produces a newer version of the same confusion.</p>
<h2>Both screens</h2>
<p>Phone and desktop are both real. A layout that is only squeezed until it fits is not a mobile site. The same content has to be reachable, readable, and tappable on the smaller screen, and still make sense on the larger one.</p>
<h2>Carry the content, then make it faster</h2>
<p>If a paragraph is still true, it moves. Rewriting everything for the sake of it throws away the words customers already search for. After the move, speed and basic SEO are part of the job: titles, descriptions, and pages that load without a speech.</p>
<h2>You should be able to edit it</h2>
<p>A rebuild that needs a developer for every sentence is a rental. The handover includes a simple way to change the text. Design stays put. Copy does not require a ticket.</p>
